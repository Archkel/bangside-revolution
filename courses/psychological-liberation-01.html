PSYCHOLOGICAL LIBERATION / LESSON 01

Communicate Clearly. Set Boundaries. Choose Deliberately.

Strong relationships and productive collaboration require more than good intentions. They require clear communication, respect for boundaries, and decisions made with awareness of consequences.

What you will complete

  • Separate observable facts from assumptions.
  • Write a clear statement about a concern or need.
  • Identify a personal boundary and a reasonable next step.
  • Create a plan for a difficult conversation.

1. Separate facts from interpretation

A fact is something you directly observed or can verify. An interpretation is the meaning you assign to it. Confusing the two can make misunderstandings harder to resolve.

Example: “The message has not received a reply since yesterday” is an observation. “They are deliberately disrespecting me” is an interpretation that may or may not be accurate.

Before reacting, ask: What do I know? What am I assuming? What information would help clarify the situation?

2. Make a clear statement

Try this structure: “When [specific behavior or event] happens, I feel or experience [impact]. I need or request [specific action].” Describe behavior rather than attacking a person's character. The other person can still disagree; clear communication does not guarantee agreement.

3. Understand boundaries

A boundary describes what you will participate in and what action you will take to protect your time, safety, or well-being. It is not a tool for controlling another person's choices.

Example: “If the conversation turns into insults, I will pause it and return when we can speak respectfully.” A boundary should be realistic, clear, and within your control.

4. Pause before a high-stakes response

When emotions run high, consider delaying a non-urgent response, checking the facts, and identifying the outcome you actually want. If someone threatens you or you feel unsafe, prioritize distance and safety over resolving the conversation.

Your communication plan

Use a real situation if it is safe to do so, or practice with a fictional example. Do not include private details, passwords, addresses, or identifying information about other people.

Completion standard

You have completed this lesson when you can distinguish an observation from an assumption, make one specific request, and describe a boundary you can reasonably maintain. You do not have to share your answers with anyone.

ECONOMIC LIBERATION / LESSON 01

TURN A SKILL INTO
A REAL OFFER.

Ownership starts with understanding what you can provide, who may need it, and how to test whether someone values it. In this lesson, you'll draft a simple offer and create your first portfolio asset.

What you'll complete

  • Identify one useful skill or service you can provide.
  • Define a specific type of customer and a problem they face.
  • Write a clear offer with a scope and a proposed price.
  • Estimate costs and choose a low-cost way to test demand.

Time: 30–45 minutes. Deliverable: a one-page offer plan.

Part 1 — Start with a useful skill

A business offer is not just an idea. It describes a specific result you can reasonably deliver to a specific person or organization. Start with something you can do now or learn to do responsibly.

Examples include formatting a résumé, editing short videos, creating a basic website, organizing a spreadsheet, designing a flyer, or tutoring a subject you understand.

Part 2 — Define the customer and problem

A broad audience is hard to serve. Choose one group and one problem. For example: independent barbers who need a simple page showing their services, location, and booking information.

Do not assume people will pay. Later, ask potential customers about the problem and test your offer before investing heavily.

Part 3 — Build your offer plan

Part 4 — Know the numbers

A proposed price is not profit. Subtract direct expenses and account for your time, platform fees, taxes, refunds, and other costs. A simple first estimate is:

Estimated amount before tax = price − direct costs − transaction fees.

This is only a starting estimate, not a guarantee of sales or take-home income. Verify the real costs for your situation.

Part 5 — Your next action

  1. Save your offer plan to your device.
  2. Ask at least three potential customers for honest feedback.
  3. Record what they actually say; do not count polite interest as a sale.
  4. Revise the offer based on evidence before spending significant money.

Completion standard: you have a written offer, defined scope, proposed price, cost estimate, and a concrete demand-test plan.
